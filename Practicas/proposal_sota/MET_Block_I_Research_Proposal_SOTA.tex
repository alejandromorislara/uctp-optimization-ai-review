\proposalheading{Problem and motivation}
{\fontsize{8.85}{10.3}\selectfont\justifying
University timetabling assigns every lecture to a period and a room while avoiding clashes involving teachers, rooms, and groups of students. A valid timetable must first satisfy these hard constraints. Its quality also depends on room capacity, the distribution of lectures across the week, curriculum compactness, and room stability. This project focuses on \textbf{Curriculum-Based Course Timetabling (CB-CTT)}, using the public UD2 formulation adopted in Track 3 of ITC-2007. Its common definition and long research history provide a shared basis for comparing different methods.\par}

\proposalheading{Scope of the review}
{\fontsize{8.65}{10.05}\selectfont\justifying
The review will examine CB-CTT research published from 2015 to September 2026, together with earlier work needed to explain the benchmark and the main algorithmic families. Searches in Scopus, Web of Science, and IEEE Xplore will be complemented by citation tracing. The analysis will cover exact and constraint-based methods, metaheuristics, matheuristics, and learning-assisted approaches. Papers on other university timetabling variants will be included only when their methods transfer clearly to CB-CTT. For each study, we will consider the formulation, data, solution method, reported evidence, computational setting, and availability of code.\par}

\proposalheading{Current and emerging approaches}
{\fontsize{8.65}{10.05}\selectfont\justifying
Recent literature presents several complementary directions. Competitive methods combine strong representations with neighbourhood search, adaptive control, decomposition, or mathematical solvers. Ceschia et al. [5] revisit SA with the \textit{LectureKick} neighbourhood and show that carefully designed local search remains competitive. Nguyen [6] uses CP-SAT to obtain a feasible timetable before applying VND. Other studies explore ALNS, matheuristics, feature-based tuning, and algorithm selection. The project will analyse these published approaches and place its implementations within that existing body of work.\par}

\proposalheading{Methods considered for implementation}
{\fontsize{8.65}{10.05}\selectfont\justifying
We intend to implement and evaluate three representative approaches: a direct CP-SAT model, a Simulated Annealing solver, and a hybrid in which CP-SAT constructs a feasible timetable that is subsequently improved by VND. The hybrid will be a focused implementation informed by Nguyen's approach. The study will use the \textbf{19 non-redundant ITC-2007 UD2 instances}. Instance \texttt{comp11} is normally omitted because competitive methods consistently reach cost zero, and \texttt{comp15} is identical to \texttt{comp03} under UD2 [1]. The practical comparison will focus on feasibility, solution quality, and running time. The precise evaluation procedure will be settled after the literature review and initial implementations.\par}

\proposalheading{Expected contribution and scope}
{\fontsize{8.55}{9.9}\selectfont\justifying
The expected contribution is a concise account of how current CB-CTT methods relate to one another, supported by transparent implementations of three representative choices. The study will clarify their assumptions, practical trade-offs, and relationship with recent research. Its scope covers literature synthesis, implementation, and comparative analysis on a public benchmark. Learning-assisted methods will be examined through the literature. The practical component will focus on established optimization approaches.\par}
